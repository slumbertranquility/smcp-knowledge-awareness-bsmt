SLIDE 02

Introduction: Communication in Global Maritime Operations

  • Seafarers from diverse cultural and linguistic backgrounds work side by side on international vessels.
  • Language misunderstandings directly link to unsafe operational situations in mixed-nationality crews Nævestad et al., 2023.
  • Maritime English coordinates routine exchanges, safety, distress, and urgency situations Sartini et al., 2025.
  • Standardized phraseology is required to eliminate ambiguity during safety-critical exchanges at sea.
This operational reality is governed by strict international and local regulatory frameworks.
Speaker Notes: Maritime operations unfold in an international environment where language barriers create severe safety risks. Standardized communication is vital to prevent bridge miscommunication.
SLIDE 03

Regulatory Foundations & The Role of IMO SMCP

  • Adopted via IMO Resolution A.918(22) in 2001 to enhance navigational safety and standardize ship communications.
  • Structured phraseology specifically engineered to prevent ambiguity in multilingual environments.
  • Linked directly to STCW competencies required for officers in charge of a navigational watch.
  • Codified locally under Joint CHED-MARINA Memorandum Circular No. 01, Series of 2023.
To measure this effectively, the study relies on established linguistic and educational theories.
Speaker Notes: The IMO established SMCP through Resolution A.918(22). This is formally integrated into STCW standards and enforced locally through Joint CHED-MARINA MC No. 01 Series of 2023.

  • Grammatical Knowledge of vocabulary, morphology, and syntax.

From these theoretical foundations, we move to the core research variables and problem structure.
Speaker Notes: Our study anchors on Canale and Swain's Communicative Competence theory, linking grammatical and strategic language skills directly to safe ship handling and error recovery.
SLIDE 05

Statement of the Problem: Knowledge Domains

Research Question 1: What is the level of knowledge of third-year BSMT students regarding SMCP?

  • 1.1 Purpose & Function — Understanding why SMCP exists and its intended role in maritime safety.
  • 1.2 Meaning & Recognition — Ability to identify and interpret selected standard maritime phrases.
  • 1.3 Terminology — Familiarity with specialized vocabulary, instructions, and warnings.
  • 1.4 Situational Application — Correct application of SMCP in basic operational scenarios.
Alongside objective knowledge, the study evaluates student self-reported awareness constructs.
Speaker Notes: The first part of our problem statement evaluates objective knowledge across four domains: purpose, phrase recognition, technical terminology, and situational bridge application.
SLIDE 06

Statement of the Problem: Awareness Constructs

Research Question 2: What is the level of awareness of third-year BSMT students regarding SMCP?

  • 2.1 Role in Standardization — Perceived awareness of SMCP's function across multilingual bridge teams.
  • 2.2 Safety Relevance — Recognition of direct links to safe navigational communication and error prevention.
  • 2.3 Shipboard Responsibilities — Understanding professional relevance to upcoming watchkeeping duties.
To ensure clarity and precision, the scope, boundaries, and terms of the study are strictly defined.
Speaker Notes: The second part of the problem statement assesses awareness—measuring how well cadets perceive the importance of standardization, safety, and their upcoming shipboard responsibilities.
SLIDE 07

Scope, Delimitations, and Definition of Terms

Scope & Delimitations

  • Population: Enrolls 3rd-year BSMT students at UCLM for AY 2026–2027; excludes BSMarE.
  • Methodology: Assesses cognitive understanding via written test instruments.
  • Exclusion: Excludes live acoustic transmission or simulator voice evaluation.

Operational Terms

  • Knowledge: Objective score on test domains.
  • Awareness: 4-point Likert scale self-reporting.
  • SMCP: Standard safety language under IMO Resolution A.918(22).
Finally, we review the practical significance and stakeholder benefits of these findings.
Speaker Notes: Our scope focuses exclusively on third-year BSMT cadets at UCLM via written instruments, setting aside live acoustic simulator tests. Key terms are operationally defined.
