SLIDE 02

Introduction: Communication in Global Maritime Operations

  • Seafarers from diverse cultural and linguistic backgrounds work side by side on international vessels, creating complex communication environments.
  • Language misunderstandings directly link to unsafe operational situations in mixed-nationality crews Nævestad et al., 2023
  • Maritime English coordinates routine exchanges, safety, distress, and urgency communications — with persistent challenges in word choice, accent, and sentence construction Sartini et al., 2025
  • Standardized phraseology is required to eliminate ambiguity during safety-critical exchanges at sea.

Communication failures on the bridge can lead to collisions, groundings, and loss of life.

This operational reality is governed by strict international and local regulatory frameworks.
Speaker Notes: Maritime operations unfold in an international environment where language barriers create severe safety risks. Nævestad et al. (2023) found that mixed-nationality crews reported more unsafe situations directly linked to language misunderstandings. Standardized communication is vital to prevent bridge miscommunication.
SLIDE 03

Regulatory Foundations & The Role of IMO SMCP

  • Adopted via IMO Resolution A.918(22) in 2001 to enhance navigational safety and standardize ship communications.
  • Structured phraseology specifically engineered to prevent ambiguity in multilingual environments.
  • Linked directly to STCW competencies required for officers in charge of a navigational watch.
  • Codified locally under Joint CHED-MARINA Memorandum Circular No. 01, Series of 2023 — aligning BSMT curriculum with operational-level competence under Section A-II/1 of the STCW Code.

Regulatory traceability: IMO → SMCP → STCW & CHED-MARINA → BSMT

To measure this effectively, the study relies on established linguistic and educational theories.
Speaker Notes: The IMO established SMCP through Resolution A.918(22) to standardize safety language. This is formally integrated into STCW standards and enforced locally through Joint CHED-MARINA MC No. 01 Series of 2023, making it a mandated component of the BSMT program.

Theoretical Framework & Communicative Competence

  • Anchored on Canale & Swain's (1980) Communicative Competence Theory and Celce-Murcia's (2007) expanded model.
  • Integrates four interrelated competencies into maritime communication analysis:
  • Grammatical Knowledge of lexical items, morphology, and syntax — reflected in SMCP phrase recognition and terminology.
  • Sociolinguistic Understanding social context and purpose — reflected in awareness of SMCP's role in safety communication.
  • Discourse Combining forms and meanings for coherent text — reflected in situational application of SMCP.
  • Strategic Compensatory strategies for repair — reflected in recognizing SMCP's professional relevance.

Mapping communicative competence domains to SMCP constructs

SLIDE 05

Statement of the Problem: Knowledge Domains

Research Question 1: What is the level of knowledge of third-year BSMT students regarding SMCP?

  • 1.1 Purpose & Function — Understanding why SMCP exists and its intended role in maritime safety communications.
  • 1.2 Meaning & Recognition — Ability to identify and interpret selected standard maritime phrases and message markers.
  • 1.3 SMCP-Related Terminology — Familiarity with specialized vocabulary, including standard instructions, warnings, and procedural language.
  • 1.4 Situational Application — Correct use of SMCP in basic maritime communication scenarios (e.g., VHF exchanges, bridge watchkeeping).

Four objective knowledge sub-domains assessed via multiple-choice test

Alongside objective knowledge, the study evaluates student self-reported awareness constructs.
Speaker Notes: The first major part of our problem statement evaluates objective knowledge across four domains: purpose, phrase recognition, technical terminology, and situational bridge application. These are measured through an objective multiple-choice knowledge test.
SLIDE 06

Statement of the Problem: Awareness Constructs

Research Question 2: What is the level of awareness of third-year BSMT students regarding SMCP?

2.1

Standardization Role

Students' perceived awareness of SMCP's role in establishing a universal communication standard across multinational maritime operations.

2.2

Navigational Safety

Recognition of the direct relevance of SMCP to safe navigational communication — preventing collisions, groundings, and operational errors.

2.3

Shipboard Duties

Perceptions regarding the relevance of SMCP to future shipboard responsibilities, particularly watchkeeping and bridge team coordination.

Measurement: Self-reported via a 4-point Likert scale measuring students' recognition of role, regulatory importance, and professional relevance. Delimited to enrolled 3rd-year BSMT cadets prior to sea deployment.

To ensure clarity and precision, the scope, boundaries, and terms of the study are strictly defined.
Speaker Notes: The second part of the problem statement assesses awareness — measuring how well cadets perceive the importance of standardization, navigational safety, and their upcoming shipboard responsibilities. This is captured through a 4-point Likert scale survey.
SLIDE 07

Scope, Delimitations & Definition of Terms

Scope & Delimitations

  • Population: Enrolled 3rd-year BSMT students at UCLM, Academic Year 2026–2027.
  • Exclusions: BSMarE students, lower-year BSMT students, and cadets already deployed onboard.
  • Methodological Boundary: Assesses theoretical/cognitive understanding via written instruments only — excludes live acoustic transmission, voice intonation, and bridge simulator performance.
  • Descriptive Design: No statistical hypothesis is tested; the study measures baseline levels of knowledge and awareness.

Key Operational Definitions

Knowledge of SMCP

Measured through objective test scores on a multiple-choice instrument covering SMCP functions, message markers, terminology, and situational phrases.

Awareness of SMCP

Self-reported score on a 4-point Likert scale measuring recognition of the role, regulatory importance, and professional relevance of SMCP.

SMCP

Standardized safety language under IMO Resolution A.918(22) for ship-to-ship, ship-to-shore, and onboard communications.

3rd-Year BSMT Students

Officially enrolled third-year students who have completed preparatory Maritime English and Maritime Communications coursework at UCLM.

Finally, we examine the practical significance and stakeholder benefits of these findings.
Speaker Notes: Our scope focuses exclusively on third-year BSMT cadets at UCLM via written instruments, setting aside live acoustic simulator tests. Knowledge is measured objectively while awareness is self-reported. Key terms are operationally defined to ensure measurement precision.
SLIDE 08

Significance of the Study & Practical Implications

⚓ BSMT Students

Provides objective self-assessment data regarding current SMCP competence, highlighting specific vocabulary and application deficiencies prior to entering mandatory shipboard cadetship.

📋 Maritime Instructors & English Faculty

Supplies diagnostic data to adjust pedagogical strategies, design scenario-based simulation scripts, and bridge the gap between theoretical English coursework and practical VHF/bridge watchkeeping communication.

🏫 University Administration (UCLM)

Serves as empirical baseline evidence for evaluating the effectiveness of current Maritime Communications (MarCom) and Maritime English instructional modules against CHED-MARINA standards.

🚢 Maritime Industry & Manning Agencies

Identifies training gaps at the pre-sea phase, supporting the industry's demand for deck cadets who can readily adapt to multi-ethnic bridge teams with minimal communication risk.

📚 Future Researchers

Contributes localized Philippine empirical data to the literature on Maritime English and provides a reference framework for comparative studies across maritime academies and programs.
